Section 1

What is predictive analytics?

Predictive analytics uses historical data, statistics and machine learning to estimate the probability of a future or unknown outcome, so that a better decision can be made. “Unknown” matters: predicting whether a past transaction was fraud is also prediction, even though it already happened.
Key wordWhat it meansSwiftTel example
Historical dataPast cases where we already know the outcome1,500 customers on 1 Jan 2025, and whether each left by 31 March
Target (outcome)The thing we want to predictchurned: Yes or No
Predictors (features)Facts we know before the outcometenure, plan type, monthly charge, support calls, satisfaction
ModelA formula learned from the data that links predictors to the targetlogistic regression (Unit 3)
ScoreThe model’s output for one new case: usually a probability“customer ST10899: 75% likely to leave”
DecisionThe action taken because of the scorecall the customer and offer a ₹300 voucher
Probability, not certaintyA weather app’s “70% chance of rain” means: on past days that looked like today, it rained about 7 times in 10. It will sometimes be wrong, and that is fine. A good prediction is one that leads to better decisions on average, not one that is always right.

Analytics is usually split into four types, each answering a harder and more valuable question. Your syllabus names three of them; diagnostic sits between descriptive and predictive. Here is SwiftTel at each step:

Section 4

Data types

Before any model, you must know what kind of data each column holds. It decides which summaries are allowed, which charts make sense and which models can use it. Getting it wrong gives nonsense such as “average gender = 0.45”.

By structure

Structured

Rows and columns with fixed meaning: our customer table, bank transactions. Ready for analysis.

Semi-structured

Has tags or keys but no fixed table: JSON from an app, XML, web server logs.

Unstructured

No predefined form: customer reviews, call recordings, photos. Must be converted to features first.

Qualitative vs quantitative

Levels of measurement (NOIR)

Psychologist S. S. Stevens (1946) described four levels. Each level allows everything the level above it in the table allows, plus one more operation:

LevelWhat you can sayExamplesValid centreAllowed mathsSPSS Measure
NominalCategories with no ordergender, plan type, churned, city namemode= ≠, counts, %Nominal
OrdinalCategories with order, gaps not equalcity tier, satisfaction 1–5, grades A/B/Cmedian+ < >, ranks, percentilesOrdinal
IntervalEqual gaps, but no true zerotemperature in °C, calendar year, datesmean+ − differencesScale
RatioEqual gaps and a true zerotenure, ₹ charge, data GB, age, support callsmean+ × ÷ ratiosScale
Two traps
  • Numbers that are not quantities. Satisfaction is stored as 1–5, but the step from 1 to 2 is not guaranteed equal to the step from 4 to 5. It is ordinal, so the median is the safe summary. A PIN code or roll number is nominal even though it is made of digits.
  • No true zero. 0 °C does not mean “no temperature”, so 40 °C is not twice as hot as 20 °C. ₹0 does mean no money, so ₹800 is twice ₹400.

Section 5

The predictive analytics lifecycle

A predictive project is a cycle: after deployment the world changes, the model slowly gets worse (model drift), and you go round again. IBM’s SPSS Modeler follows the same idea under the name CRISP-DM.

#StageKey questionSwiftTel
1Define the problemWhat decision will the prediction improve? How is success measured?Reduce churn with a limited voucher budget
2Collect dataWhich past cases, with known outcomes, are available?Customer snapshot on 1 Jan + who left by 31 Mar
3Prepare dataAre types, missing values and errors fixed?Yes/No → 1/0; fill 45 missing satisfaction scores
4ExploreWhat patterns does descriptive analysis show?Churn rises with support calls, falls with tenure
5Build modelWhich technique fits the target?Logistic regression on 70% of customers
6EvaluateDoes it work on data it has never seen?Test on the other 30%: lift 2.16
7DeployHow are scores used in daily work?Weekly call list for the retention team
8MonitorIs it still accurate? When do we retrain?Compare predicted vs actual churn every month

Read the signs: each support call raises the risk; longer tenure, autopay and higher satisfaction lower it. The model turns these into a probability with p = 1 / (1 + e−z), where z is the weighted sum. You will build this yourself in Unit 3; for now, play with it in Lab Part B.

Evaluate: on customers the model never saw

Test accuracy             : 78.4%

Why accuracy is not enoughOnly 22.7% of customers churn, so a lazy “model” that always says No is already 77.3% accurate. Our model’s 78.4% barely beats it. Its real value is ranking: the 10% it rates riskiest churn at 48.9%, more than twice the average (lift = 48.9 ÷ 22.7 ≈ 2.16). Units 3–4 add precision, recall and ROC curves.

Offer to all 1500 customers instead  : expected net Rs -68,497
Do the maths: expected value A ₹300 voucher; 40% of would-be leavers accept it and stay 6 more months.
Expected gain = p × 0.40 × (6 × monthly charge) − 300
Customer with p = 0.70 on a ₹399 plan: 0.70 × 0.40 × 2,394 − 300 = +₹372 → offer.
Break-even risk: p = 300 ÷ (0.40 × 6 × charge) = 0.31 for ₹399 but only 0.125 for ₹999. High-value customers are worth saving even at lower risk, which is why the top of the call list is mostly postpaid.

The same lifecycle also answers a time-series question: next quarter’s revenue. A straight trend line through 24 months gives revenue = 482.81 + 5.664 × t, so January 2025 (t = 25) ≈ ₹624.4 lakh.

SPSS · Lab Experiment 1: import, define variables, set measurement levels

  1. File → Import Data → CSV Data, choose swifttel_customers.csv, keep “first line contains variable names”, finish.
  2. Switch to Variable View (tab at the bottom). Each row is one column of your data.
  3. Check Type: text columns are String, numbers Numeric, join_date should be Date.
  4. Set Measure using the table in Section 4: Nominal for gender, plan_type, autopay, churned, customer_id; Ordinal for city_tier and satisfaction; Scale for age, tenure_months, monthly_charge, data_gb, support_calls.
  5. For satisfaction, click Values and add labels: 1 = Very unhappy, 2 = Unhappy, 3 = Neutral, 4 = Happy, 5 = Very happy.
  6. Run Analyze → Descriptive Statistics → Frequencies on churned and plan_type. Check you get 22.7% Yes.
Why Measure matters in SPSS SPSS uses it to decide which charts and procedures to offer. A variable marked Scale gets a histogram and a mean; one marked Nominal gets a bar chart and counts. Mark satisfaction as Scale by mistake and SPSS will happily report a mean that may mislead.

Section 8

Summary and revision

TopicRemember this
BasicsHistorical data + statistics/ML → probability of a future or unknown outcome → better decision. A probability, not a certainty
Types of analyticsDescriptive (what happened) → diagnostic (why) → predictive (what will happen) → prescriptive (what to do). Value and difficulty rise along the way
Key mathsRate = part ÷ total; trend ŷ = a + bt; p = 1/(1+e−z); lift = rate in top group ÷ overall rate; expected gain = p × benefit − cost
ApplicationsChurn, credit scoring, fraud, demand forecasting, predictive maintenance, readmission, insurance pricing, campaign response, attrition
Data typesStructured / semi-structured / unstructured; qualitative (nominal, ordinal) vs quantitative (discrete, continuous); cross-sectional, time series, panel
Measurement levelsNominal (mode) · Ordinal (median) · Interval (mean, no true zero) · Ratio (mean, true zero, ratios). SPSS: Nominal, Ordinal, Scale
LifecycleDefine → Collect → Prepare → Explore → Model → Evaluate (on unseen data) → Deploy → Monitor (drift) ↺
ToolsPython (pandas, scikit-learn), R (glm, lm), Excel (FORECAST.LINEAR, Solver), SPSS (menus, Measure)

Key terms (click a card to flip)

Target variableclick
The outcome a model predicts, e.g. churned (Yes/No).
Predictor (feature)click
A column known before the outcome, used to predict it, e.g. support calls.
Churnclick
Customers leaving a company. Churn rate = customers who left ÷ customers at the start.
Liftclick
How many times better than random a model’s top group is: 48.9% ÷ 22.7% ≈ 2.16.
Ordinalclick
Ordered categories with unequal gaps (satisfaction 1–5). Use the median, not the mean.
Ratio scaleclick
Numeric with a true zero, so ratios make sense: ₹800 is twice ₹400.
Train / test splitclick
Learn on one part (70%), judge on unseen data (30%) to get an honest score.
Model driftclick
A deployed model getting worse because behaviour changed. Why we monitor and retrain.

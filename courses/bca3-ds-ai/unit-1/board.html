Everyone is predicting you

🌧️ Weather app: 70% chance of rain tomorrow
🏦 Bank: will this student repay an education loan?
📱 Telecom: will this customer switch networks next month?

“70%” means: on past days like this one, it rained 7 times in 10.
The past, used carefully, estimates the future.

Basics

What is predictive analytics?

Using historical data, statistics and machine learning to estimate the probability of a future or unknown outcome, so that a better decision can be made.

Output is a score / probability (0.70 = 70% likely), not a guarantee.

Predictive · maths

Predictive 2: a churn probability

z = −1.08 − 0.035·tenure + 0.0012·charge
    + 0.398·calls + 0.635·prepaid
    − 0.584·autopay − 0.244·satisfaction
p = 1 / (1 + e−z)    (always between 0 and 1)

Weights were learned from 1,050 past customers.

Prepaid · 8 months · ₹399 · 4 support calls · no autopay · satisfaction 2
z = 0.857
p = 1 / (1 + e−0.857) = 1 / 1.4245 = 0.70
odds = 0.70 / 0.30 ≈ 2.4 : 1
Loyal: 40 months, autopay, 0 calls, satisfaction 5 → p ≈ 0.02
Evaluation · maths

Is the prediction any good?

Train on 1,050 customers (70%) · test on 450 unseen ones (30%)
Accuracy = correct ÷ total = 78.4%
“Always say No” = 1 − 0.227 = 77.3% 😮

When one answer is common, accuracy flatters.

Top 10% riskiest (45 customers):
22 churned → 22 ÷ 45 = 48.9%
Lift = 48.9% ÷ 22.7% = 2.16×
Calling the model’s top list finds 2× more leavers per call than calling at random.
Prescriptive · maths

Prescriptive: what should we do?

Offer a ₹300 voucher? 40% of would-be leavers accept and stay 6 more months.

Expected gain = p × 0.40 × (6 × charge) − 300
Our customer (p = 0.70, ₹399):
0.70 × 0.40 × 2,394 − 300 = +₹372 → offer
Break-even p = 300 ÷ (0.40 × 6 × charge)
₹399 plan: 0.31 · ₹999 plan: 0.125
StrategyExpected net
Offer nobody₹0
Offer all 1,500−₹68,497
Offer the 455 with gain > 0+₹93,591

Prediction says who is at risk. Prescription adds cost and value to choose the action.
